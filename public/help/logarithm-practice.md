# Logarithm Practice

## Overview

This study aide gives you random logarithm problems in base 10, base 2 and base e, like "What is log₁₀(0.01)?" or "Write log₂(8x³) in terms of log₂(x)." Type your answer to check it, or reveal the answer and a step-by-step method.

The logarithm base b of a positive number y is the power of b that gives y:

- log_b(y) = r means the same thing as bʳ = y.
- So log_b(bʳ) = r. For example, log₁₀(1000) = 3, log₂(1/8) = −3 and ln(√e) = 1/2.
- ln is the natural logarithm, the logarithm base e (e ≈ 2.718).

To work with expressions you also need the rules of logarithms (for positive A and B, and any power n):

- **Product rule:** log_b(AB) = log_b(A) + log_b(B).
- **Quotient rule:** log_b(A/B) = log_b(A) − log_b(B).
- **Power rule:** log_b(Aⁿ) = n·log_b(A). Roots are powers too: √A = A^(1/2), ∛A = A^(1/3).

## Choosing What to Practice

Click a button to turn it on or off. Changing any choice starts a new question.

### Bases

**log₁₀** and **log₂** (on by default) and **ln** (off by default): which logarithms appear. At least one base stays on.

### Practice

**Numbers** (on by default): evaluate the logarithm of a number. The number is always a power of the base in disguise, such as 1000, 0.01, 1/100, 8, 0.25, 1/e², or 16^(3/4). The answer is a whole number or a fraction.

**Expressions** (off by default): use the rules of logarithms. Most questions ask you to write something like log₁₀(100x³), log₂(x/8), ln(x²y) or log₁₀(√(10x)) in terms of log₁₀(x) (and log₁₀(y)). Some ask you to simplify the log of an exponential, such as log₂(8ᵗ) or ln(e^(2t+1)). The variables x and y are positive.

**Roots & fractions** (on by default): include square roots, cube roots and fractional powers, such as log₂(√8), ln(1/√e), log₁₀(10√10), 8^(2/3) and log₁₀(√(100x)). When it's off, every answer to a Numbers question is a whole number.

At least one of Numbers and Expressions stays on.

## Components

**Question:** The problem.

**New question:** Get a new random problem. Your answer, the feedback and the revealed sections are cleared.

**Your answer:** Type your answer here, then press Enter or click **Check**. The feedback box next to it says whether you're right:

- **✓ Correct!** Any equivalent form counts: 3/2 and 1.5 are both correct, and so are 2 + 3 log10(x) and 3log10(x) + 2.
- **✗ Not quite.** If your answer matches a common mistake (a sign error, counting zeros instead of decimal places, multiplying by the root instead of dividing, turning log(AB) into log(A)·log(B), forgetting the constant, (log x)³ instead of 3 log x, and so on), the feedback names it.
- **≈ Close:** your answer has the right value but isn't finished. For example you used a rounded decimal (0.667 instead of 2/3), you left a log of a number (log₁₀(100) instead of 2), a log still has more than just x inside, or you used a different base than the question.
- **Read as:** shows how your typing was understood, so you can spot typos.

**Show answer:** Reveals the answer, with a decimal for fractions.

**Show method:** Reveals the steps. For a number: write it as a power of the base, then take the logarithm. For an expression: roots become powers, then the quotient rule, the product rule and the power rule, then evaluate the logs of numbers, and finally multiply out.

## Typing Your Answer

- **Numbers:** whole numbers, fractions or exact decimals: `-2`, `3/2`, `-1/3`, `1.5`. Fractions like 2/3 must be fractions; 0.667 is only close.
- **Logarithms:** `log10(x)`, `log2(x)` and `ln(x)`. You can also type `log_10(x)`, `log_2(x)`, `log₁₀(x)`, `log₂(x)`, or leave out the parentheses: `ln x`. A plain `log(x)` means log₁₀(x).
- **Multiplication:** `*`, a space, or nothing: `3*log10(x)`, `3 log10(x)` and `3log10(x)` all work.
- **Fractions in front of a log:** `(1/2)*ln(x)`, `ln(x)/2` or `0.5 ln(x)`.
- **Exponent problems:** answers are in terms of t, such as `3t` or `2t + 1`.

Use lowercase x, y and t.

## What to Observe

- With **Numbers** and base 10, compare log₁₀ of 1000, 1 and 0.001: numbers bigger than 1 have positive logs, 1 has log 0, and numbers between 0 and 1 have negative logs.
- For a decimal like 0.001, the log is minus the number of decimal places (−3), not minus the number of zeros.
- With **Roots & fractions**, notice that a square root halves the logarithm: log₂(√8) = (1/2)·log₂(8) = 3/2.
- With **Expressions**, check that the product rule turns multiplication inside the log into addition outside it, and the power rule turns powers inside into multiplication outside.
- Compare log₂(8ᵗ) with log₂(2ᵗ): writing 8 = 2³ gives 8ᵗ = 2³ᵗ, so log₂(8ᵗ) = 3t.

## Study Tips

1. Work out each problem on paper before checking or revealing.
2. For numbers, always ask: "what power of the base gives this number?" Rewrite the number as a power of the base first.
3. For expressions, peel off the outside first (a root, then a fraction bar, then products), and use the power rule last.
4. If you're stuck, reveal only the method, then try typing the answer yourself.
