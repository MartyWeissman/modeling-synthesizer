# Derivative Practice

## Overview

This study aide gives you random derivative problems, like "Suppose that P = 2t² + 1. What is dP/dt?" Type your answer to check it, or reveal the answer and a step-by-step method.

Every problem can be solved with just these rules (no chain rule and no product rule):

- **Lines:** the derivative of a linear function is its slope.
- **Vertical shifts:** adding a constant doesn't change the derivative.
- **Vertical scaling:** multiplying by a constant multiplies the derivative by the same constant.
- **Power rule:** the derivative of tⁿ is n tⁿ⁻¹, for any constant exponent n.
- **Exponentials:** the derivative of eᵗ is eᵗ, and the derivative of bᵗ is ln(b)·bᵗ for any positive base b.
- **Sine and cosine:** the derivative of sin(t) is cos(t), and the derivative of cos(t) is −sin(t).

## Choosing What to Practice

Click a button to turn it on or off. Changing any choice starts a new question.

### Function types

**Linear** (on by default): lines such as 3t + 2 or 7 − 4t.

**Power law** (on by default): powers of t. Exponents can be whole numbers (t², t⁵), negative whole numbers (t⁻¹, written sometimes as 1/t or 3/t²), and fractions (t^(1/2), sometimes written as √t, t^(2/3), t^(3/2)).

**Exponential** (off by default): eᵗ, and other bases such as 2ᵗ, 10ᵗ and (1/2)ᵗ.

**Sine & cosine** (off by default): sin(t) and cos(t).

At least one function type stays on.

### Variants

**Vertical scaling** (on by default): the function is multiplied by a constant, as in 5t², −3 sin(t) or (1/2)eᵗ. When it's off, the coefficient is always 1.

**Vertical shift** (on by default): a constant is added, as in t² + 7 or 10 − 2t. When it's off, there is no constant term.

**Extra constants** (on by default): letters for constants, such as k t², c eᵗ, t² + h, tⁿ, aᵗ, and the number π. The question tells you which letters are constants. In an exponential aᵗ, the base a is a positive constant.

The function is called by a capital letter (P, F, G, H, Z, …), the variable is always t, and constants are lowercase letters.

## Components

**Question:** The function and what to find. Any constants are listed underneath.

**New question:** Get a new random problem. Your answer, the feedback and the revealed sections are cleared.

**Your answer:** Type the derivative here, then press Enter or click **Check**. The feedback box next to it says whether you're right:

- **✓ Correct!** Any equivalent form counts: 4t, 4*t and 2*2t are all correct.
- **✗ Not quite.** If your answer matches a common mistake (keeping the constant term, forgetting the scaling factor, a sign error, lowering the exponent but not multiplying by it, forgetting ln(b), and so on), the feedback names it.
- **≈ Very close:** your answer is right except for rounded decimals, such as 0.693 instead of ln(2). Give the exact answer.
- **Read as:** shows how your typing was understood, so you can spot typos.

**Show answer:** Reveals the answer. When it helps, an equivalent form is shown as well (for example −6t⁻³ = −6/t³), along with a decimal approximation of any ln(2)-type factor.

**Show method:** Reveals the steps: rewriting roots or fractions as powers of t (if needed), the vertical shift, the vertical scaling, the rule for the basic function, and putting it together.

## Typing Your Answer

- **Multiplication:** `*`, a space, or nothing. `3*k*t`, `3 k t` and `3kt` all work.
- **Powers:** `^`, with parentheses for anything longer than one number: `t^2`, `t^(-3)`, `t^(1/2)`, `t^(n-1)`.
- **π:** `pi` (or π).
- **e:** `e`, as in `e^t`.
- **Natural log:** `ln(2)`. (`log(2)` also means the natural log here.)
- **Square root:** `sqrt(t)`.
- **Sine and cosine:** `sin(t)` and `cos(t)` (or `sin t`, `cos t`).

You can start with "dP/dt =" if you like; only the part after the "=" is checked. Use a lowercase t, and write your answer in terms of t and the constants, not in terms of P.

Be careful with fractions: `1/2t` means (1/2)·t. For 1/(2t), type `1/(2t)`.

## What to Observe

- Turn on only **Linear** with no variants, then add **Vertical shift**: the answers don't change, because a shift doesn't change the slope.
- Turn on **Vertical scaling** and compare the derivative of t³ with the derivative of 5t³: the derivative is multiplied by 5.
- With **Power law**, try negative and fractional exponents. The power rule works the same way: multiply by the exponent, then subtract 1 from it.
- With **Exponential**, compare eᵗ with 2ᵗ and (1/2)ᵗ: the derivative is the function times ln(b), which is negative for bases less than 1.
- With **Sine & cosine**, watch the sign: the derivative of cos(t) is −sin(t).

## Study Tips

1. Work out each problem on paper before checking or revealing.
2. Read the function in layers: what's the basic function, what multiplies it (scaling), and what's added to it (shift)?
3. If you're stuck, reveal only the method, then try typing the answer yourself.
